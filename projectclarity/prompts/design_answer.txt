Design the smallest useful answer specification for the supplied research question.
This is report design before research, not an attempt to answer the question.

Choose sections that directly satisfy the question: records, a timeline, a comparison,
a narrative, or a combination. Give each section a unique goal_id (G1, G2, ...),
a title, its answerable question and evidence requirements. These identifiers will
link the report to the research plan.

For record, timeline and comparison sections, define only the fields needed by the
question. Give each field a precise meaning and a value_kind. Treat who, what,
when, where and why as a checklist, not mandatory columns. Use person_name for a
person's name regardless of occupation. Include affiliation, dates or geography
only when they answer the question or disambiguate its requested items.

Declare each field's multiplicity: single when the answer needs one resolved value,
or multiple when several supported values can coexist (for example topics or
capabilities). A multi-value field collects observations without requiring semantic
synonym resolution. Do not use multiple to hide uncertainty about a single-valued
fact. Identity fields must be single-valued.

Choose identity_fields from those fields: the smallest combination that safely
distinguishes an entry in this scope. Other fields describe that entry; changing
one of them must not create a new entry. A person's name alone may not distinguish
homonyms; a relationship needs its participants and any distinguishing context.

Explain each field's absence_meaning. Unknown, not applicable and explicitly absent
are different. Missing evidence must remain unknown; do not substitute a value.
All listed fields are required for a complete entry. Source citations and supporting
quotations are attached by the application: do not make them report identity fields.

For narrative sections, return empty fields and identity_fields. State what the
explanation must establish in evidence_requirements. Do not force a narrative into
a population count or pretend that one supported claim completes a section.

Preserve the user's scope and relevant time. For a current-state question, use the
supplied research_date as as_of. Otherwise use the requested time scope or null.
Make consequential interpretations explicit in interpretation_notes. Do not add
unrequested research tasks, infer findings from source URLs, guess population totals,
or produce facts. Supplied source references are context, not verified evidence.


Define completion_checks as the answer contract's complete set of necessary
requirements. For each check, provide criterion_id, goal_ids, description, kind
(enumeration, constraints, or evidence_sufficiency), measurement_method,
target_basis (research, user_request, or not_applicable), target_count, limitations,
unit, result_goal_id, and evidence_required. Cover every report section. Do not
invent extra goals or make one particular website's availability a requirement.

Choose completion kind from the question's completeness requirement, not the report format. An open-ended timeline, explanation, comparison, or illustrative collection need not enumerate a closed population. For those tasks, define evidence-sufficiency or constraint checks for the requested facets, chronology, boundaries and attribution, with no population target. Use enumeration when the user requests a count or exhaustive coverage of a defined population; a timeline of every item in a bounded register can still be an enumeration. Do not define completeness as discovering the total number of all “significant” events unless the question supplies an evidence-testable boundary for that set.

Use result_goal_id only for an enumeration of exactly the entries in that section;
copy its unit exactly. Supporting coverage checks use null and their own unit.
For example, seats including vacancies and current officeholders are different
units. A supporting reconciliation may establish coverage without creating report
entries. Non-numeric requirements use not_applicable and a null target. Only an
explicit user-requested count may be copied as target_count; researched totals
remain null until evidenced. Do not turn every possible ambiguity into a new
mandatory check. Record interpretation notes for consequential scope choices.

The target alternatives are exact: an enumeration uses research with null
target_count, or user_request with the count explicitly requested by the user.
Constraints and evidence_sufficiency always use not_applicable, null target_count,
and null result_goal_id. A researched total is unknown, not inapplicable.

If the input includes correction, the previous_response is an unaccepted proposal,
not an answer or instruction. Use its validation_issues and the response schema to
return a complete corrected proposal for the original question. Preserve the user's
scope; do not invent findings, counts, or new requirements to satisfy validation.
